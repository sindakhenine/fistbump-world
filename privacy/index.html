Who is responsible

This is the controller for the personal data described here under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). We are not required to appoint a data protection officer.

What we collect and why

When you sign in with X, X shares with us, with your permission:

  • your X account ID, name, @handle, profile photo and bio;
  • your follower and following counts, account creation date and the location text on your profile;
  • your email address, if X provides it;
  • whether you and other Fistbump members follow each other on X, checked only for people who are themselves Fistbump members.

We use this to create your account, show your profile, and place the right people on your map (contract, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). We never post, follow, or send messages on X for you, and we don’t collect data about people who aren’t Fistbump members.

Your X connections: for each pair of Fistbump members, we store whether each of them follows the other on X and when we last checked. We check again every few days, or when you tap Update from X. We never read or store your full list of followers or people you follow. We use these connections to decide who appears on your map and who can find you, and to show how you know each other (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

What you add: the country and optional city you choose (never a street address or GPS), your character’s look, interests, bio, who may find you, trips and free times you announce, pings, invitations, messages, meetup confirmations, events and RSVPs, blocks and reports. We use these to run the service you ask for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Profile photo: if you upload your own profile photo, it is stored in Fistbump’s photo storage (Supabase, London) and shown, like your X photo, to the members who can see you, for example on the map, on your profile and on the Season board. Like your X photo, it has a public web address, so anyone who has that address can open it. It is deleted when you change it or delete your account (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Moods and moves: you can give your character a mood (for example happy or coffee) or a move (for example dance or say hi). Members who can see you on the map see it until it runs out, after 15 minutes to 24 hours. You pick from a fixed list, with no free text, and we keep only your latest choice (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Who you met: your number of Fistbumps (confirmed meetings) is shown to the members who can see you. Who you met stays private: only you and the person you met see it. If you both turn on Show who I’ve fistbumped, members who can see both of you see that you two met, on your profiles. Either of you can turn it off at any time, and it never shows to people you blocked or who blocked you (Art. 6(1)(a) and (b) GDPR).

Fistbump news: if you turn on Show my Fistbumps in Fistbump news, members who can see you on the map are shown, for 30 days, that you have been meeting builders. They never see with whom, on which day or where. It is off unless you turn it on, and never shown to people you blocked or who blocked you. You find both settings under your picture → Privacy → Manage fistbump privacy (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR).

About me: what you’re building, what you’re looking for and what you can offer, your skills, the languages you speak and whether you’d like to find a cofounder. It is shown on your profile to the members who can see you. About 30 days after you change what you’re building, looking for or can offer, we may remind you once to check it (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

My apps: the apps you add: name, pitch, who it’s for, the problem it solves, category, tags, description, stage, links (website, App Store, Google Play and test access), a logo and up to 4 screenshots. A published app is shown on your profile to the members who can see you, and the logo of the app you choose is on your character’s shirt. When you list an app in the app directory, every member can find it; members who can’t see you on the map see it without your name. Anyone with the link can open its public page (app.fistbump.world/a/…), which shows its name, logo, pitch, stage, category and links, but not test access, your name or your profile. Logos and screenshots are kept in Fistbump’s logo storage (Supabase, London) with a public web address, like your profile photo, also for drafts. That address contains your Fistbump account number, so someone who knows it can tell which apps are yours. When you remove a picture, the app stops showing it; its file is deleted when you upload a logo in My apps an hour or more later, when you delete the app, or when you delete your account (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

What members say about apps: you can mark an app Want to try, Tested it or Using it; the builder and other members see only the totals, never who. You can write one review of an app: a rating, your text and, if you like, where you used it, the version and what you tried to do. A review is shown to the members who can see that app. Your name is shown with it only if you choose so and the reader can see you on the map; otherwise it says “a Fistbump member”. The builder can reply once, shown with the review. When a review or reply is reported, the Fistbump team sees who wrote it, can hide or remove it with a reason that only its author sees, and keeps a record of that decision. Hidden and removed reviews stay stored so their author can see why: a hidden one can be edited to ask the team to look again, a removed one can only be deleted. A review goes when its author deletes it, or with the app or the author’s account (Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR).

App numbers: when a member opens an app’s page, we count one view per member, app and day (the builder’s own views don’t count). To count each member once, we store a one-way code made from the member and the app with a key that changes every day; codes and keys are deleted shortly after midnight (UTC). We keep only daily totals per app, and delete those after about 400 days. The builder sees the totals (with Plus, week by week), never who (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Profile numbers (Plus): when a member opens the profile of a member who has Plus, opens their project’s website or one of their apps, or taps the logo on their character, we count it for that member: once per viewer, profile and kind of visit a day. Nothing is counted for members without Plus, and visits by the member themselves, by the Fistbump team and by members set to Hidden don’t count. To count each viewer once, we store a one-way code made from the viewer, the profile and the kind of visit with a key that changes every day; codes and keys are deleted shortly after midnight (UTC). We keep the day’s totals until that night, then only weekly totals, and delete those after 8 weeks. The member sees this week’s and last week’s totals, never who, and a day’s visits only from the next day, so a number can’t be matched to one visit (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Finding builders and saved searches (Plus): a Plus member can list the builders they can already see on the map by what those builders wrote in About me (Looking for, Can offer, Building, skills and languages). A search isn’t stored unless the member saves it. A saved search (the need, the words and the language) is kept until the member deletes it or their account. It sends an alert when a builder who matches, and who lets everyone find them, shows up in the member’s town or turns on Available today near them (only if their Available today reaches the member). Builders who let only their network find them are never part of an alert. We keep a record of each alert (the builder, the search and when), so the same builder never comes twice and there are at most 3 a day, also after a search is deleted and saved again; it is deleted with either account (Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR).

Similar apps and topic notices: we compare apps’ categories, tags and words to show similar apps, with the reason. If you turn it on for your app, or follow a problem area, we send you a notice once a day about new matching apps and keep a record of what we sent, so you don’t get the same one twice. If you tell us a match is wrong, we keep your note to improve the matching (Art. 6(1)(a) and (b) GDPR).

Feedback swaps and build sessions: when you suggest a meeting to swap feedback or to build together, we store its purpose, the apps you’ll try, the platform, the minutes, what feedback would help and a task you share. Only the two of you see them. Feedback you write after a swap is seen only by the two of you, and a private note on a session only by you (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Available today: if you show that you’re available, we store what for, your town, an optional area you type (please don’t type a street address), a point near the town centre rounded to about 1 km, until when, and who may see it. The members your choice reaches, among those who can see you, see it until it ends; it is deleted within 10 minutes after it ends. If you turn on alerts, we tell you when someone you can see becomes available, or when trips overlap your town or your trips, and keep a record of what we sent so you don’t get the same alert twice (Art. 6(1)(a) and (b) GDPR).

Who’s in town (Plus): if you turn on “Tell Plus builders in the same town when I visit” (off until you do; it also needs “All approved builders” and meeting requests from new builders), Plus members outside your X network who live in a town you visit, or go there on the same days, learn your name, that town and the dates of trips you share with all your connections or everyone, in a notification and in their Plans; never the place, your note or trips you keep to fewer people (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). A trip you share with everyone can reach any builder who can find you on the map, with this switch on or off. If you have Plus, we tell you about such visits to your town and your trips, at most 3 a day, unless you turn that off in Plans. For each alert we keep who it was for, which trip, when and whether it went out, so nobody hears about the same trip twice, until a month after the trip ends (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Events (Event Radar): we list public events for builders. The Fistbump team finds them on public web pages and posts, by hand or with a research tool, and stores each event’s title, organizer, town, times, a short summary and links to its sources (a source can be a public post on X); nothing about Fistbump members. A person on the team checks every event, and every event members suggest, before others see it. If you mark an event Interested or Going, we keep it until 90 days after the event. Members see how many of their mutual connections marked it, and a community total shown only from 3 people; you are counted anonymously in community totals unless you turn that off (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The list of who’s going shows your name only if you choose so: to your mutual connections, with or without Plus, or also to Plus members who can find you on the map, outside your X network too; they can then ask to meet you there if you accept meeting requests from new builders. Others see only the totals. If you let members follow your event activity, those who follow it, with or without Plus, get a notice naming you when you mark an event; you see how many follow it, not who. Events you suggest are shown without your name and stay listed after you delete your account; we keep who suggested an event, when, and your note to the team, never shown to other members and deleted with your account. If you are named as an organizer and want it corrected or removed, write to us (Art. 6(1)(a), (b) and (f) GDPR).

Opportunities: cards about problems people still need solved, drafted by the Fistbump team from public posts and pages (by hand or with a research tool) or suggested by members, and checked by the team before others see them. You can save a card, say you have the problem, are researching it or are building something (others see totals only), add a source (shown without your name once the team accepts it) and comment. A comment shows your name and picture to the members on the map; if you hide from discovery, only to members who can see you. A comment you withdraw, or the team hides, stops showing but stays stored until you delete your account. You can link your listed app to a card; we keep who linked it and when, and readers see the app and its link. Cards you suggest, and sources the team accepted into a card, stay without your name after you delete your account. With Plus you can follow topics for a daily notice (Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR).

Your own assistant (Connect Grok): if you connect an assistant, for example a Grok Bot, you choose what it may read: your Fistbumps, your plans, events, your apps, builders you can find, and your saved opportunities. While it is connected, it can read these parts from Fistbump, and its provider (for example xAI for Grok) receives what it reads under its own privacy policy. This can include details about other members that you can see in Fistbump: the names and handles of people you met or invited, and the name, handle, town, interests, project and bio of builders you can find. It never reads messages. We store the connection (the assistant’s name, what you allowed, when it last read something) and count its reads to limit them; Supabase handles the sign-in. Turn it off at any time under your picture → Privacy, and it can’t read anything after that (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR; for other members’ details that you can already see, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Sharing: the picture you share for an app, an event, an opportunity or a meeting is made on your device and isn’t stored by Fistbump. The text goes to X only if you open X’s post window, or to the app you pick in your phone’s share sheet (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Limits: to stop abuse, we note when you use some features (for example saving an app, writing a review or suggesting an event). A daily clean-up deletes notes older than 2 days, so they stay up to 3 days, also after you delete your account (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Fistbump Park (games on the website and in the iPhone and Android apps): when you play, we store each game (who played, the rules picked, the moves and scores needed to run it, the result, and why it ended if it ended early, for example that someone left), your ladder points, tier, wins, losses, draws and streaks for each game and for all games, and a history of how your points changed, also from games against the computer. The people in a game see that game and its chat: the quick phrases and the short messages players type (up to 200 characters, no links). A message someone reports is kept with the report, so the team can read what was sent. In Lucky Joker, where the cards are hidden, our server deals and keeps each player’s hand and shows it only to that player until the round is counted. Members who can see you on the map can see your ladder points and record. Only you see your history and your record against your connections, grouped by how you know each other on X and person by person. Inviting someone to a game sends them a notification; an invitation expires after 10 minutes (live games) or a day (turn games), and a game nobody plays for a while ends on its own. Games never count for the Season (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Private game rooms and your game history (Plus): a Plus member can make a room and invite friends; we store the room (who made it, the game, the rounds), who was invited and whether they joined, said no or left, and the room’s games and ranking. The people in a room see each other’s name, photo and character, who is invited, the pairings and the ranking, but not the name or photo of someone either of them blocked. Your game history (your wins, losses, favourite games and the games you played) is made from your games; Free members see the last 10 games in the app, and Download my data has all of it. Rooms and the history never count for the Season or the ladder (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Plus character items: we store the last free persona, hat and entrance you chose, and a Plus item put away when Plus ends, so it goes back on if you renew (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Voice in games: in a game with friends you can tap Join voice to talk. Your microphone is used only after you join, and only until you leave the voice. Your voice goes directly from your device to the other players’ devices, encrypted end to end; we never record or store it. To connect, the devices exchange connection details through our servers, so the other players’ devices learn your IP address. When devices can’t reach each other directly (often on mobile networks), the encrypted voice is relayed by Cloudflare, which cannot listen to it (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Language: we store the language you choose on your account, so the app and your notifications reach you in it (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Notifications: we keep a list of updates for you (for example someone wants to meet, said yes, wrote to you, invited you to a game, or is visiting your country). If you turn on notifications, we store your browser’s push address or your phone app’s push token so we can deliver them, and delete it when you turn them off (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

The Season: if you join the Season competition, your name, photo, country and points are shown to other members on the Season board, as described in the Season rules. Points are counted from meetings both people confirmed, once one of them added a live fistbump photo taken in the app and that photo passed the check below. When you confirm a meeting you also give the country it happened in; it decides whether the meeting counts as home (1 point) or as a trip (8 points) for you. If the Season has an award, you tell us when you join where you’d like to go and when, so the award fits; only the Fistbump team sees this. After a Season ends, we do the Season check: we may check the accounts and meetings, and the live photos from their meetings in that Season, of the top 3 and of every member who would get a prize box under the Season rules (places 1 to 6 in Season 1, the winner from Season 2 on). We do this before we announce a winner. If a box later passes to the next member in the ranking, that member gets the same check before the box is theirs. We keep whether a checked member passed. We do this to keep the competition fair (Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR).

The photo check (Season): every live fistbump photo taken in the app for a meeting that falls in a Season is checked, usually within a day, so that Season points are given only for meetings that happened. Unless you or the person you met chose a check by a person (see “Your choice” below), the photo is sent to Claude, an AI model run by Anthropic. We use it under Anthropic’s Commercial Terms of Service and its data processing addendum. Under those terms the contracting company for customers in the European Economic Area is Anthropic Ireland, Limited (Ireland). It acts as our processor and uses other companies of its group and further sub-processors, which it lists at trust.anthropic.com/subprocessors.

What is sent and what the model does: we send the picture and nothing else: no names, handles, ids, dates, places or the pose, and the copy we send carries no location or other metadata. The model answers three fixed questions: how many clear faces the photo shows, whether a fistbump is visible, and whether it looks like a real camera photo rather than a generated, edited or re-photographed one. It is told not to describe or identify anyone, and we store only the verdict and a short reason code, never the model’s words. The check counts faces; it never matches a face against your profile photo or anyone else’s, and no biometric template is made. It does not confirm who is in a photo and does not prove that a photo is genuine. Before the photo is sent, our own server compares a coarse fingerprint of the image (64 bits, not a face) with earlier meeting photos to spot a photo used twice; that fingerprint stays with us.

Where the photo goes and how long it is kept there: Anthropic stores what it receives in the United States, and we set every request so that the model runs in the United States only. Anthropic’s sub-processors, which it lists on the page linked above, may be in other countries. Where the photo leaves the European Economic Area, the EU’s standard contractual clauses in Anthropic’s data processing addendum apply. Under its terms Anthropic may not train its models on the photo. According to its published retention rules (as we read them on 13 November 2026), Anthropic deletes what it receives through its interface for developers within 30 days. It may keep a photo longer, up to 2 years, if its automated safety systems flag it as a breach of its usage policy, or where the law requires it. We have not agreed a shorter period with Anthropic. A photo that is checked a second time after a request for a review is received by Anthropic a second time, and the same periods apply to that copy.

What the result changes: the result gives or withholds provisional Season points for both people in the photo. An AI model can make mistakes. If a photo is not accepted, both people are told why, can take one new live photo together within 7 days, and can then ask for a review once. A review starts with a second automatic check: the photo is sent to Anthropic one more time, in the same way and under the same terms, and a stronger Claude model answers the same three questions. If the photo passes, the points count and both people are told in the app. If it is refused again, or the second check cannot run, a person on the Fistbump team opens the photo and decides; that is logged like every other photo access. A team member can also open a photo that passed the second check and overrule it. If you or the person you met chose a check by a person, there is no second automatic check: the photo is not sent again and a person decides. A verdict of the model that nobody questions stands without a person having looked at it, so a refused photo can lower a member’s place in the ranking. The model does not choose a winner. Before a Season’s winner is announced, a person on the team answers every review that was asked for in that Season, unless the second automatic check already accepted the photo, and people on the team check the top places as described under “The Season”. If you think a refusal was wrong, ask for the review: unless the second automatic check accepts the photo, a person then decides.

Your choice: you can object to the AI check at any time and without giving a reason. In the app: your picture (top right) → Privacy → Season photo check → Have a person check my photos; the same switch is shown where you confirm a meeting and where you take the photo. From then on the live photos of your meetings, including photos another member takes with you, are not sent to Anthropic, and they are not sent for a second check when a review is asked either. A person on the Fistbump team opens and checks them instead (logged like every other photo access), which can take longer than a day. Your points and your place in the Season stay the same. A photo that was already sent cannot be called back. We look your choice up again right before each photo is sent; a photo that is already on its way at the moment you switch this on may still be checked by the model once, and we then discard the model’s answer and a person decides. Our own comparison with earlier meeting photos (the fingerprint described above) runs for these photos too, on our server. We store that you made this choice and when, until you change it or delete your account. Only the two people who met should be in the photo; Fistbump is for adults, so keep children and other people out of the picture.

We do this to keep the Season fair and to prevent made-up meetings, which is our legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The choice above is how you use your right to object (Art. 21 GDPR).

Meeting photos: a photo you add of a meeting is stored privately and shown only to the two of you. Others see it on your profiles only if you both approve that photo and you both show who you’ve fistbumped; either of you can withdraw that at any time, and it never shows to people you blocked or who blocked you. We remove location and other metadata from the picture before it is uploaded. Live photos taken with the in-app camera record the pose you were asked for and the time. Photos are deleted with your account (Art. 6(1)(a) and (b) GDPR). The Fistbump team opens a private meeting photo only to review a report about it, the meeting or one of you (while the report is open or up to 30 days after it was handled), for the Season check described above, or to decide on a Season photo check (when you or the person you met chose a check by a person, when you ask for a review of a refused photo, or when the automatic check could not give an answer). Each time is logged: who on the team opened which photo, why (the report or the Season) and when. The log keeps member and photo IDs, not the photo, for 2 years, and is then deleted. Your data download lists each time the team opened one of your photos (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Payments: on the website, Stripe processes your payment for Fistbump Plus. We receive your Stripe customer ID, your plan, its status and when it renews or ends, never your card details. In the iPhone and Android apps, Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play) processes it. Usage: we note when you last opened Fistbump, to count active members, and the app shows how many builders are online right now as a single number, without saying who (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). To know how many people visit, the website and the app add one to a daily total for the page you open and, while a page is open, one a minute to a count of people online right now. These totals hold nothing about you: no cookie, nothing stored in your browser for them, and no IP address or other identifier is kept with them. Daily totals are deleted after 25 months, the online count after an hour (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Some totals are public at app.fistbump.world/stats and on this website: people online now, visits today, and how many members are on the map, their countries, Fistbumps, meetups agreed, games played together and times members said they are going to an event. Age and terms: when you join, we store when you confirmed that you are 18 or older and agreed to the terms, and which version of the terms, so we can show it later (Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR).

Voice relay: when your device asks for access to the voice relay in a game, we note the time and the game, to limit how often one member can ask. We delete this within a day and an hour (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Your website’s icon: if you ask Fistbump to use your website’s icon as an app’s logo, our server looks up your website’s address with Cloudflare’s public DNS service and loads the website’s start page and icon. Only the website address is used for this, nothing about you (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

Technical logs: when your browser or app talks to our servers, Supabase records technical details of the request (such as the IP address, time and what was asked for) to keep the service running and secure. These logs are deleted automatically after a short period set by Supabase (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Safety: reports and blocks are used to keep members safe and to act on abuse. A report can include a copy of what was reported, so the team can read it (legitimate interests, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Contact form: when you write to us at fistbump.world/contact, we store your email address, your name if you give it, your message and when you sent it, so we can read and answer it. The team sees it on our admin page and also gets it by email (through Resend, see below). We delete it from Fistbump after 6 months; the copy in our mailbox is kept like any email you send us, as long as we need it to answer you. To stop the form being misused, we keep a code made from your IP address, which can’t be turned back into the address, for about a day; we never keep the address itself (Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR).

Who can see what

Only approved members see the member map, and only according to your settings: by default just your X connections who use Fistbump, or also other approved builders if you allow it, or nobody if you hide. Your location is shown at country or city level only. Your mood, your move, your Fistbump news and your Fistbump Park ladder follow the same settings, and who you met and your meeting photos also need the choices described above. Messages are visible only to the two people in the conversation. Exact event venues are shown only to guests who are going, unless the host marks the venue as a public place.

Apps you list in the app directory, reviews, Event Radar events and opportunities are shown to all members on the map, as described above, and a listed app’s public page to anyone with its link. A member’s own assistant reads only what that member can see in Fistbump, in the parts they allowed.

World Partners are businesses that pay for a logo marked “Sponsored” on the member map. To give them honest monthly totals, we count when a member sees a partner’s logo (at least half of it on screen for a second), opens its profile or taps “Visit website”. We keep who counted for one day only, so each member counts once per half hour, then only daily totals per logo. Partners never see names, photos, handles, places or anything else about who saw them (legitimate interests, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). A partner’s website has its own privacy policy.

Monthly World Partner reports: on the 1st of each month we email the contact of each running placement its totals for the month before, with a new private link to its page. We keep when each email was sent and the provider’s reference for it, for 25 months (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

World Partner applications: when a business applies at app.fistbump.world/sponsor, we store its name, website, social links, category, description, logo and pictures, the contact’s name and work email, and the country and VAT number it is registered under, to review the application, run the placement and invoice it (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR; invoices are kept as tax law requires, Art. 6(1)(c)). Pictures stay private until we approve them. Payments go through Stripe. We delete an application on request, and within 12 months if no placement follows; earlier travel partner applications are kept under the same rule.

World Partner sign-in: a business signs in or starts its application at app.fistbump.world/sponsor with an email address and a 6-digit code, without an account. We email the code to the address that was entered, whether it has an application or not. We keep that address for one day with the code in a form that can’t be read back, and a code made from the sender’s IP address, to limit how many codes go out. When a code confirms an address that has no application yet, we keep that for about 2 hours, so the application can say its email was confirmed, and the application then keeps when that happened. A signed-in device is remembered for 30 days, or until you sign out (Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR).

Services we use

  • Supabase stores the database and photos, runs our server functions and handles sign-in, hosted in London, United Kingdom (Amazon Web Services). The EU has decided that the UK protects personal data adequately (adequacy decision, renewed until 2031). Our X access tokens are stored encrypted.

  • Resend (Resend, Inc., USA) sends the World Partner emails (sign-in codes, the monthly report and the emails about an application or placement) and passes messages from the contact form on to us, from its EU region (Ireland). Cookies and storage

We don’t use advertising or analytics cookies. Your browser stores your sign-in session and a few settings, such as the game level you chose, your sound and volume settings and your language, so the app keeps working between visits. If you try the map before you sign in, a picture you pick for your character stays in your browser only.

How long we keep data

We keep your account data while your account exists. When you delete your account, your profile, photos, X connection checks, requests, messages, announcements, events, Fistbumps, moods and Fistbump Park ladder and games are deleted, and so are your apps with their logos and screenshots, your reviews, replies and declarations, your notes and feedback, Available today, your Interested and Going, the activity you follow, your opportunity saves, signals, sources and comments, your assistant connections, your saved searches with their alert records, and your profile numbers. Events and opportunity cards you suggested stay without your name. Reports may be kept as long as needed to handle them, and invoices as long as tax law requires (usually 10 years in Germany).

Some things go earlier: Available today within 10 minutes after it ends, Interested and Going 90 days after the event, app view codes shortly after midnight, app view totals after 400 days, profile visit codes shortly after midnight, weekly profile numbers after 8 weeks, and the notes we keep to stop abuse within 3 days. Events are deleted 400 days after they end, and messages from the contact form 6 months after they were sent. The verdict of a Season photo check (valid or not, and the reason code), and of a second check after a request for a review, stays with the meeting it belongs to. Anthropic normally deletes the copy of a photo it received within 30 days; “The photo check” above lists the exceptions. Your choice of a check by a person is kept until you change it or delete your account. Our backups run behind: a weekly encrypted backup of stored files can hold a deleted photo for up to about 97 days after you deleted it, the weekly copy of the database (which holds verdicts and the log of photo openings, not photos) is kept 90 days, and our database provider’s daily backups are kept 7 days.
